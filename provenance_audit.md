# Place Pulse 2.0: provenance audit of the tie response

This document records, in a form that can be checked independently, the
evidence that the released Place Pulse 2.0 comparison data were generated by a
response model that differs from the one described in the dataset paper.

Every claim below is either (a) quoted from a public source with a content
hash, or (b) reproducible with a named script in this repository.

---

## 1. The dataset paper does not describe ties

Dubey, Naik, Parikh, Raskar & Hidalgo (2016), *Deep Learning the City: 
Quantifying Urban Perception at a Global Scale*, ECCV 2016.
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.01769>

Section 3 describes the task as a forced choice between two images and states
the learning problem with `y ∈ {+1, -1}`. **The published description contains no
tie category, no third response option, and no image-retirement rule.** The
paper states that Place Pulse 2.0 used "the exact same data collection
interface" as Place Pulse 1.0.

Place Pulse 1.0 (Salesses, Schechtner & Hidalgo, 2013, *PLoS ONE* 8(7):e68400,
<https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068400>) **does** document the option, in
one sentence of its Methods:

> "Users additionally had the option of indicating that both images were
> perceived as equal."

So the response category was designed in and documented once, and then dropped
from the description of the successor dataset while remaining in the data.

## 3. The data carry the signature of that rule

All four checks below are reproduced by `scripts/diag_last_vote.py` and
`scripts/diag_deactivation.py`. Numbers are for the safety question
(506,165 responses, 111,389 images, 48,920 voters).

| Check | Result | Expected if no retirement |
|---|---|---|
| Tie rate of an image's **last** response | **35.6%** | equal to its other responses |
| Tie rate of its **other** responses | **9.6%** | — |
| Images with exactly 2 responses that have exactly 1 tie | **88.8%** | ~22% at p = 0.125 |
| Images with exactly 2 responses that have **no** tie | **0.2%** | ~76.6% at p = 0.125 |

The last two rows are the decisive ones. Under independent sampling a 2-response
image should show no tie about three times in four (0.875 squared, 76.6%);
instead almost every such image has exactly one tie, and almost none has zero.
That is the distribution produced by ending an image's record at a tie.

A further consistency check: at a 12.5% tie rate the expected number of
responses before a tie is about 8. The observed median responses per image is
9.

## 4. What is and is not established

**Established.** The response category exists; the platform code retires images
on ties; the released data show the retirement signature; the dataset paper
does not describe any of this.

**Not established, and not claimed.** Whether the rule was active for the whole
of the 2013-2016 collection window or only part of it; how many images were
retired in total (the released tables do not carry the `active` or `equal_to`
fields); and whether Place Pulse 1.0 used the same rule (its ~100 responses per image are
consistent with the rule not operating there, though responses per image also
depend on pool size and collection budget, so this is suggestive rather than
decisive).

**Consequence for users.** Retirement truncates an image's record rather than
removing it from the released tables: 78.7% of the released images took part in
at least one tie. Since ties are strongly associated with particular raters
(Section 5), how many responses an image accumulated before its record ended was
not independent of who happened to rate a pair.

## 5. Why this matters: ties track the rater, not the scene

Reproduced by `scripts/tie_sources.py`, `scripts/defense_checks.py`,
`scripts/rater_tie_transfer.py`, `scripts/six_attributes.py`.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Tie prediction AUC from rater identity | 0.812 |
| Tie prediction AUC from image content | 0.532 |
| Tie prediction AUC from pair composition (same city) | 0.502 |
| Tie propensity ICC across raters (6+ responses) | 0.263 |
| Left/right preference ICC across raters | 0.086 |
| Within-quarter tie ICC (controls for pool drift) | 0.263 |
| Rater tie style transfer, other cities → this city | r = +0.51, positive in 56/56 cities |
| Correlation of tie rate with binary-choice inconsistency | ρ = +0.006 (p = 0.29) |

The last row is the one that separates a tie threshold from a general noise
parameter: if tie use were merely a low signal-to-noise rater, tie rate would
covary with binary-choice inconsistency. It does not.
